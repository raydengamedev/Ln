# The Demon Lord's Army Is Bankrupt, So I Was Summoned as Its Accountant

**Short title:** *The Demon Lord's Accountant*
**Genre:** Isekai fantasy · workplace comedy · political intrigue · slow-burn romance
**Format:** Light novel series, about 50,000 words per volume, 5 volumes planned
**Tone:** Roughly 60% comedy, 40% intrigue. A dry, deadpan first-person narrator.
**Comparable titles:** For readers who like competent heroines rebuilding broken systems (*Ascendance of a Bookworm*), governance isekai (*How a Realist Hero Rebuilt the Kingdom*) and economics in fantasy (*Spice and Wolf*).

---

## Logline

A forensic auditor is summoned to save the Demon Lord's army. She can't fight, but she can follow the money. When she does, she finds the war is a business, and the Church on the "good" side is lending to both armies.

## Back-cover blurb

> Kurata Tsumugi has spent five years finding money that other people hid. She was three hours from closing the biggest audit of her career when a summoning circle opened under her desk.
>
> The Demon Lord wanted a warrior. He got her.
>
> His army hasn't been paid in seven months. His castle is half sold off. And in ninety days, a loan his father signed comes due, with the entire Ashen March as collateral.
>
> Tsumugi can't swing a sword. But she can read a contract, follow the money, and spot a lie in a column of numbers from across the room. The deeper she digs into the Demon Lord's debt, the clearer it gets: someone has been making a fortune from this war for a very long time.
>
> They should have checked who they were summoning.

---

## The core idea

Most isekai solve problems with power. This one solves them with **paperwork**, and treats it as just as dramatic.

The joke at the start (an accountant in a fantasy war) slowly turns into the real theme. **Wars run on money, and whoever controls the money decides when the war ends.** The Demon Lord's army isn't losing because it's weak. It's losing because it's broke, and someone made sure it would be.

Each volume pairs an **audit** (a mystery: where did the money go?) with a **crisis** (a battle, a mutiny, a deadline). Tsumugi solves the crisis by solving the audit.

## Protagonist

### Kurata Tsumugi, 27, Grand Treasurer (eventually)

- **Before:** a forensic auditor at a mid-sized Tokyo accounting firm. She's the one they send when the numbers "feel wrong." She's blunt, deadpan and a workaholic, and she has never once rounded a figure.
- **Why she does it:** when she was fifteen, her father's small printing business went under after his partner quietly drained it. Everyone said he was "bad with money." He wasn't. He'd been lied to, and nobody checked. Tsumugi became the person who checks.
- **Her belief:** *"Numbers are how you find out who's lying, and who's being lied to."*
- **Her flaw:** she trusts figures more than people, and she does everything herself. Her arc runs from lone auditor to someone who builds a team and lets others hold the books.
- **Her one Earth item:** a solar-powered calculator. It never runs out of power under this world's sun. The demons think it's a holy relic, and she has stopped correcting them.

### Her skill: [Double Entry]

On her status screen it looks pathetic: *Class: Auditor. Strength: 3. Skill: [Double Entry].*

**What it does:** in double-entry bookkeeping, every debit has a matching credit, so nothing is taken without something being given. When Tsumugi carefully examines a document, contract, transaction or statement, [Double Entry] shows her a faint red mark wherever **something was taken without something being given back**.

**Its limits (which keep her competence front and centre):**
- It only tells her *that* something doesn't balance, never *what*, *why* or *who*. She still has to do the investigation.
- It only works on things she has studied closely. She can't just glance across a room and spot crime.
- Heavy use gives her splitting headaches. The longer the audit, the higher the cost.

**How it grows:** in later volumes she can sense imbalance in bigger systems: a trade route, a city's economy, and finally the war itself, which she comes to see as one huge unbalanced entry.

## Main cast

### Azhrael Vel-Noct, Ninth Demon Lord of the Ashen March
About 300 years old, which is young for a demon. Nine feet tall, horns like a ram's, slate-grey skin, a voice like a landslide trying to be polite. He inherited the throne three years ago, and his father's debts with it.

He's sincere, physically terrifying, bad at social situations and **catastrophically bad with money**. He has been paying his soldiers out of his own treasury, and when that ran out, by selling off the castle furniture. He sold his throne to pay for the summoning circle.

- **Arc:** from a ruler who avoids bad news to one who reads everything he signs.
- **With Tsumugi:** a slow-burn romance. He's the first person who listens when she says "this doesn't add up." She's the first person who tells him the truth without being afraid of him.
- **Running gag:** each chapter, the castle is a little emptier.

### Grisla Two-Tusk, Orc Quartermaster
An 80-year veteran who has kept the army's supply records the whole time, in tally sticks and her own memory. At first she's openly hostile to Tsumugi ("A human with a magic counting box? Spy.") and later becomes her fiercest ally.

- **Secret:** she can't read, and she's ashamed of it.
- **Payoff:** in the Volume 1 climax, her perfect memory of eighty years of shipments becomes the key testimony that breaks the case.

### Nib, Imp Clerk
Small, eager and anxious. He becomes Tsumugi's assistant and later her apprentice. Imps have eight fingers, so **imps count in base 8**, which causes total chaos in the records. Underestimated by everyone, Tsumugi included at first.

### Sileth Marr, Dark Elf Treasurer
Elegant, polite, very helpful, and clearly hiding something. Tsumugi catches him skimming from the treasury in the first act.

- **Twist:** he's been skimming to **pay pensions to the widows and orphans of dead soldiers**, after the army quietly stopped paying them. He's a thief, but not the villain. Tsumugi brings him onto her side, and he becomes her moral counterweight.

### Cardinal-Treasurer Oswin Thale, House of Saint Obol
The main villain of the first arc. He runs the banking arm of the **Church of the Gilded Scale**, the dominant faith of the human kingdoms. He's gentle and grandfatherly, and he truly believes endless war is a stable, God-given economy. He never raises his voice and never loses his temper. He just raises the interest rate.

### Haruto Ikeda, the Hero (introduced at the end of Volume 1)
A 17-year-old summoned by the Church as the human side's Hero. Exhausted, brave and being exploited. He's about to find out the Church has billed the kingdom forty times the real cost of his own summoning. He starts as a rival, then becomes a reluctant ally, and he's the main co-lead in Volume 2.

### The Notary of the Stone of Oaths
An ancient, neutral, sphinx-like being who enforces every sworn contract in both realms. Demons and humans alike can't break a sworn contract. **The Notary is this world's court of law**, which makes contracts the most powerful weapons in it.

## World and rules

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| **The Ashen March** | The demon realm: volcanic borderlands, proud and poor. Currency: *marks*. |
| **The Holy Kingdom of Aurelis** | The largest human kingdom. Currency: *crowns*, minted by the Church. |
| **Church of the Gilded Scale** | The dominant faith. Its symbol, a set of scales, stands for divine justice. Its bank, the **House of Saint Obol**, issues war bonds to human nobles. |
| **Grayhallow Trading Company** | A "demon-friendly" merchant house that lends to the Demon Lord. Secretly owned by the House of Saint Obol. |
| **Coinmarch** | A neutral trade city on the border where both sides do business. |
| **Sworn contracts** | Magically binding. Breaking one has real, physical consequences. Only the Notary can void one, and only if it was **dishonest at signing**. |
| **The Reckoning Moon** | The end of the fiscal year, when debts come due. A natural deadline for every volume. |
| **Mixed number systems** | Imps count in base 8, dark elves in base 12, orcs in tally marks, humans in base 10. The army's books are a mess of conversions, and fraud hides in the gaps between them. |

### The central scheme
The Church makes money from the war on **both sides**:
1. It sells war bonds to human nobles and collects interest and "holy war" tithes.
2. Through Grayhallow, it lends to the demons at ruinous rates, so they can never win and never quite lose.
3. If the demons ever default, the collateral (**the Ashen March itself**) passes to the lender. In other words, the Church wins the war by **foreclosure**, not by battle.

An endless war is a steady income. Peace is bad for business.

## Volume 1 outline: *Opening Balance*

**Ticking clock:** 90 days until the Reckoning Moon, when the loan payment is due. Default means the Church takes the Ashen March.

Chapter titles are accounting terms, which becomes a series signature.

**Prologue: The Last of the Treasury**
*Azhrael's point of view.* He performs the summoning in an empty throne room, having sold the throne to pay for the circle. "Come forth, one who can save my army!" Light, smoke, and a woman in an office suit holding a calculator, in the middle of a sentence: "—why don't you *foot*?"

**Chapter 1: Opening Balance**
Tsumugi in Tokyo at 2 a.m., closing a year-end audit, then pulled into the other world. Her pathetic status screen. Azhrael explains that the soldiers haven't been paid in seven months. She asks for the books. Grisla: "What books?"

**Chapter 2: Source Documents**
A tour of the half-empty castle. The army's records turn out to be tally sticks, base-8 imp scrolls and elegant base-12 dark elf ledgers, none of which agree. She meets Sileth and adopts Nib. She works three days without sleep building one master ledger. [Double Entry] lights up all over the place.

**Chapter 3: Payroll**
An ogre regiment threatens to desert. Tsumugi finds hidden value: 4,000 barrels of fine wine that were wrongly written off as "cursed" (the "curse" was a filing mistake). She sells them in Coinmarch and makes a partial payroll. Paying a regiment of ogres turns out to be a logistical comedy. **First win:** the soldiers start calling her "the Ledger Witch."

**Chapter 4: Material Misstatement**
She finds the loan. The terms are monstrous: interest compounds every moon phase, and the collateral is the realm itself. Azhrael admits he never read it; his father signed it. Ninety days. The stakes are now clear.

**Chapter 5: Reconciliation**
About 3% of every payment disappears when it's converted between number systems. Tsumugi and Nib trace it, and the trail leads to Sileth. A tense confrontation.

**Chapter 6: Related Parties**
*The twist.* Sileth takes her to the villages and shows her the widows and orphans he has been paying. An emotional turning point: Tsumugi sees the people behind the numbers. Sileth joins her, and the two of them realise the real drain isn't theft inside the army. It's the interest paid to Grayhallow.

**Chapter 7: Due Diligence**
Tsumugi, Nib and a disguised Azhrael go undercover in Coinmarch to investigate Grayhallow. She sees the human side for the first time: their soldiers aren't being paid either, while Church criers sell war bonds in the square. She briefly crosses paths with a tired young man in a hero's cloak (Haruto). **Midpoint reveal:** a Grayhallow sales record shows the summoning circle was sold to Azhrael **deliberately sabotaged** to bring "one who has never held a weapon." The circle obeyed both conditions: someone who has never held a weapon, *and* someone who can save the army.

**Chapter 8: Going Concern**
The Church now knows about her. Assassins strike the castle, and Grisla holds the door. Afterwards, Grisla's secret comes out. Tsumugi's response: "Your memory is a better ledger than most I've audited." On the roof, Azhrael confesses he sold the last chair in the castle that morning. A quiet, warm scene between them.

**Chapter 9: Audit Opinion**
Building the case. Under sworn-contract law, the Notary can void a contract that was dishonest at signing. [Double Entry] shows the original loan never balanced. The proof is in the treasury vault: unspent coins from the loan's first shipment, which turn out to be **debased crowns with only 60% of their stated silver**. Grayhallow only ever handed over 60% of the money it lent. Each ally has a role: Nib's conversions, Sileth's records, Grisla's memory.

**Chapter 10: The Reckoning Moon**
*Climax, on two fronts.*
- **At the Stone of Oaths:** Tsumugi's audit hearing against Grayhallow's lawyer, the Cardinal's proxy. A courtroom duel of evidence. The turning point is Grisla's spoken testimony of shipment counts, eighty years exact.
- **At the mountain pass:** Church knights march to seize the "collateral" as the moon rises. The ogre regiment that almost deserted in Chapter 3, now paid, holds the line. *An army that's been paid shows up.*

The Notary rules: the debt is reduced to the value actually delivered, and the predatory interest is void. The debt is now payable. The Ashen March is saved.

**Epilogue: Subsequent Events**
Tsumugi is appointed Grand Treasurer, and the castle gets its first new chair. Then she reads Grayhallow's seized papers. Owner of record: **the House of Saint Obol, Church of the Gilded Scale**. They have been lending to both sides all along.

The last scene: a letter, smuggled across the border.
> *"I heard the demons have a Japanese accountant. I need help. They're charging me for my own summoning. — H. Ikeda, Hero"*

## Series arc (5 volumes)

| Vol. | Working title | Audit | Crisis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | *Opening Balance* | The Demon Lord's debt | Foreclosure on the Ashen March |
| **2** | *The Hero's Invoice* | The Church's billing of the Hero and the human kingdom | A temporary truce. Tsumugi has to audit the enemy, with Haruto as her uneasy partner. |
| **3** | *Trade War* | Supply chains and currency | The Church cuts off salt and iron. Tsumugi reforms the demon currency and founds a central bank. |
| **4** | *The Vault of Saint Obol* | The Church's own books | An infiltration of the Holy See's vault, revealing the Church owes debts of its own to something older. |
| **5** | *Closing the Books* | The war itself | A peace negotiation and a final hearing against the Church before the Notary, with both nations watching. Peace becomes cheaper than war. Tsumugi chooses whether to go home. |

**Series theme:** *Money is a record of who trusted whom.* The war continues because someone profits from people not checking. Tsumugi checks.

## Running gags and signature elements

- **The castle gets emptier,** and later fuller again as the finances recover. It's a visual measure of the series' progress.
- **The holy calculator.** Demons bow to it. A cult forms around it in Volume 2.
- **"Receipt?"** Tsumugi won't approve anything without documentation, including a dragon's request for a hoard allowance.
- **Number-system chaos.** Nib's base-8 panic attacks.
- **Chapter titles are accounting terms,** which works as a recognisable series brand.
- **Azhrael tries to be romantic using financial vocabulary** he picked up from her. ("Your eyes are... a material asset.")

## Sample passage: Chapter 1 opening

> The thing about fraud is that it's almost never clever.
>
> People picture embezzlers as criminal masterminds: offshore accounts, forged signatures, a second set of books hidden behind a painting. In five years at Takamine & Partners, I'd found exactly one second set of books, and it was in a desk drawer labeled SECOND SET OF BOOKS.
>
> Most fraud is just someone counting on nobody checking.
>
> I checked. That was the whole job, and I was good at it.
>
> Which is why, at 2:14 a.m. on the last night of fiscal year-end, I was the only person left on the eleventh floor, three energy drinks deep, staring at a variance of ¥4,200,000 that had no business existing.
>
> "Why don't you foot," I told the spreadsheet. "Why don't you *foot*."
>
> The spreadsheet didn't answer. The floor did.
>
> It started glowing.
>
> ---
>
> The first thing I saw in the other world was a man the size of a vending machine, kneeling in front of me.
>
> He had horns. Real ones, curling back from his temples like a ram's. His skin was the grey of wet slate and his eyes were like two coals someone had forgotten to put out. Behind him, an honor guard of what I can only describe as armored nightmares raised their weapons.
>
> "Great one," the horned man said, in a voice like a landslide trying to be polite. "Summoned across the void with the last of my treasury. I beseech you. Save my army."
>
> I looked at him. I looked at the nightmares. I looked down at what I was still holding: a solar-powered calculator and half a can of something that promised to give me wings.
>
> "What's wrong with your army?" I asked.
>
> He hesitated. "They have not been paid in seven months."
>
> And that, God help me, was the moment I got interested.

## Market and KDP notes

- **Length:** about 50,000 words per volume (roughly 200–230 pages at a 5" × 8" trim), which is standard for a light novel.
- **Release strategy:** having Volumes 1 and 2 ready before launching makes the series more credible and helps read-through from one volume to the next.
- **Suggested KDP categories:** Fantasy › Humorous · Fantasy › Action & Adventure · Fantasy › Romantic · Fantasy › Coming of Age (or the closest current equivalents).
- **Keyword ideas:** isekai light novel, summoned to another world, demon lord, female protagonist isekai, fantasy comedy, slow burn fantasy romance, kingdom building, competent heroine.
- **Cover concept:** Tsumugi in her office suit, calmly holding the calculator up like a relic, with Azhrael towering behind her. They stand in a half-empty throne room with an ogre army in the background. The long title runs across the top in the usual light-novel style.
- **Before you commit:** search Amazon and light-novel databases for "accountant isekai" and "demon lord finance" to check how crowded the niche is. The concept is original, but the market should be checked.
